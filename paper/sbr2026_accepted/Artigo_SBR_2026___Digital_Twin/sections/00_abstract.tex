\begin{abstract}
Robot soccer teams in the RoboCup Small Size League develop and validate control software largely in simulation, where simplified physics leaves a reality gap to the field. This paper presents a behavioral model of an omnidirectional soccer robot identified from the competition logs that teams already record. The proposed approach models each Degree of Freedom (DoF) as a First-Order-Plus-Dead-Time (FOPDT) channel. The translational channels identified directly from in-game logs reach roughly an 87 percent hold-out fit. 

The identification of the yaw channel required a short, dedicated open-loop campaign to restore the excitation and identify the channel using a 71 percent hold-out fit. The digital twin's fidelity is evaluated in free-run over horizons of 0.1 to 1.5 seconds against an ideal-kinematics baseline using an identical harness.  Over 73.5 seconds of scored motion, the model reaches a median position error of 0.039 meters at the 0.4-second horizon, compared to 0.101 for ideal kinematics and 0.129 for rSim, an ODE simulator widely used across the league.
\end{abstract}
