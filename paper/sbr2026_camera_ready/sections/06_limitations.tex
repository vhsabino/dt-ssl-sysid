\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Data and identification}

The open-loop yaw fit rests on six linear-regime events (four training, two
held out) from a single session, without a fine amplitude sweep between $0.5$
and $1.0\,\si{rad/s}$, so the commanded-yaw threshold is reported as the
interval $0.58$--$0.60\,\si{rad/s}$ rather than a point. All data come from one
robot and one context, so no cross-robot or cross-session variability is
reported; pre-registered criteria and deterministic re-execution mitigate this
but do not replace replication. The two yaw campaigns differ in loop topology,
in the ratio of commanded yaw to commanded translation, and in session. The
dilution analysis of Sec.~\ref{sec:results:omega} rules out the simplest
alternative, that the actuation threshold merely switches yaw off (it would
hold for only ${\sim}2\%$ of competition samples), but cannot separate that
ratio from feedback, because the dedicated collection removes both at once;
separating them needs an open-loop arm with translation superimposed, which we
did not collect. We also diagnose the bias of direct prediction-error identification on
closed-loop data without attempting the closed-loop-aware estimators
(instrumental-variable, two-stage, joint input--output) that the cited
literature proposes. The Ljung--Box test rejects residual whiteness for both yaw
fits; as pre-registered it is informative rather than a gate, since the
smoothing differentiator colors residuals by construction. Finally, the
firmware-implied and CAD-measured radius ratios differ by ${\sim}3.3\%$, and an
inherited body-frame dead-zone constant of $0.3\,\si{rad/s}$ has no recoverable
provenance and is superseded here.

\paragraph{Evaluation and scope}
The hold-out is overwhelmingly translational, so the rotational fidelity of the
twin, as opposed to the yaw estimate itself, is not validated against a
distribution resembling competition play. % CORTE 2026-08-09: prosa apertada; a ressalva "fitted vs. untuned" e o
% indicio de que parte do deficit do rSim vem do harness permanecem.
The comparison against rSim is
between a plant fitted to this robot and an engine left at default parameters,
so it bounds what an untuned reference simulator delivers on this task rather
than establishing a general dynamical advantage; the zero-parameter
ideal-kinematics baseline also outperforms rSim at the reference horizon, which
indicates that a substantial share of rSim's deficit comes from the replay
harness rather than from its physics. The matched-delay ablation rules out the
transport-delay term as the source of the gap, but does not separate the fitted
dynamics from the residual initial-velocity mismatch, which likewise grows with
horizon. All results come from a single dataset and hold-out split drawn from
the same operating regime; the native grid is irregular and the free-run initial
condition relies on a vision-derived velocity that is noisy, mitigated by
resampling and filtering but not eliminated.
